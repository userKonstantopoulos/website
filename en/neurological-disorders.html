Stroke · Aphasia · Dysphagia

Assessment and rehabilitation in neurological disorders.

Specialized speech therapy care for communication and swallowing disorders after a stroke, traumatic brain injury or degenerative neurological disease.

Clinical goals

The intervention focuses on functional communication, swallowing safety when dysphagia is present, and supporting the person and the family in everyday life.

  • Assessment of language, speech and communication.
  • Investigation of swallowing difficulties when there are signs.
  • Design of an individualized therapeutic plan.
  • Collaboration with the family and caregivers where needed.

How rehabilitation is organized

In neurological cases, therapy is adapted to the person's functional profile: what they can understand, how they express themselves, how clear their speech is and how safe their swallowing is. Goals are introduced gradually, with an emphasis on everyday communication.

Speech and communication disorders

The assessment examines how the difficulty affects everyday communication, comprehension, expression, articulation and speech clarity.

  • Aphasia and language difficulties after a neurological event.
  • Dysarthria and reduced speech clarity.
  • Apraxia of speech and difficulty with motor planning.
  • Cognitive-communication difficulties.

What functional communication means

Therapy does not target isolated words or sounds only. It aims to help the person express needs, take part in conversation, use strategies when they struggle and communicate more effectively in their environment.
